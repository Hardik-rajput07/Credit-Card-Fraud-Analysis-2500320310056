Problem Statement



Credit card fraud is rare but costly, and it is hard to spot because fraudulent

transactions are mixed in with a very large number of normal ones. Banks need

simple rules that flag suspicious transactions, but they also need to know how

many real frauds those rules catch and how many genuine customers they wrongly

flag. This project studies a public credit card transaction dataset to find

fraud patterns, builds rule-based flags, and checks them against the labelled fraud.



Objectives

1\. Clean the dataset and log every fix, with zero duplicates left.

2\. Build at least 3 fraud rules (unusual amount, unusual hour, velocity).

3\. Compare flagged transactions with labelled fraud and report precision and recall.

4\. Build a Power BI dashboard with a flagged-transaction view and at least 3 slicers.



Business Questions

1\. What share of transactions is fraud, and how much money is lost?

2\. At which hours and amount ranges is fraud most common?

3\. Which merchant categories and locations have the highest fraud rates?

4\. How many frauds does each rule catch, and how many of its flags are real fraud?

5\. Which cards make unusually fast repeated transactions, and are they more likely fraud?
